## Part 1 — The Story Bank (your answer to 80% of questions)

Most behavioral questions are variations of a few themes. Build **6 STAR stories**
once and you can answer almost anything.

**The themes to cover (one strong story each):**
1. A time you led or influenced others
2. A time you solved a hard problem
3. A time you failed or made a mistake (and what you learned)
4. A time you handled conflict or a difficult person
5. A time you delivered under pressure / a deadline
6. Your proudest measurable achievement

**The build prompt:**
```
Help me build a STAR story for the theme: [theme]. Here's what happened: [dump
the rough details]. Structure it as Situation, Task, Action, Result — 60–90
seconds spoken. Make the Result quantified and the Action clearly MINE (not
"we"). Keep it conversational, not memorized-sounding. Then list the 5 other
common questions this same story can answer.
```

## Part 3 — The Answer Frameworks (so you never ramble)

**For behavioral ("Tell me about a time…"):** STAR — Situation, Task, Action,
Result. Spend most time on **Action** (what *you* did) and **Result** (the
number).

**For "What's your weakness?":** name a *real* but non-fatal weakness, then the
concrete system you use to manage it. (Never "I'm a perfectionist.")

**For "Why should we hire you?":** pick the 2 requirements they care about most →
match each to a proof point → close with the value you'd add.

**For "Tell me about yourself":** Present (who you are now) → Past (the 1–2
experiences that got you here) → Future (why this role is the logical next step).
90 seconds, no life story.

**The framework prompt:**
```
Here's my draft answer to "[question]." Tighten it to under 90 seconds spoken,
keep my voice, cut filler, sharpen the result, and flag anything that sounds
rehearsed or arrogant. Then ask me one follow-up an interviewer might.
MY DRAFT: [paste]
```

## Part 4 — The Live Mock Interview (the highest-value rep)

Do this the day before. It removes most anxiety because you've already "been
there."

```
You are an experienced interviewer for [job title] at a [type of company]. Run a
realistic 25-minute interview. Ask ONE question at a time and wait for my answer.
After each, give brief, specific feedback (what landed, what to tighten) before
the next. Mix behavioral, role-specific, and culture-fit questions. Increase
difficulty as we go. Start with "Tell me about yourself." Begin now.
```

**Run it twice.** The second time, ask it to "be a tougher, more skeptical
interviewer who probes for specifics."

## Part 5 — Closing strong + the questions you ask them

**Always have 3–4 sharp questions ready.** Asking good questions signals you're
evaluating *them* — that's what strong candidates do.

```
I'm interviewing for [role] at [company]. Give me 6 thoughtful, specific
questions to ask my interviewer that show genuine insight — not "what's the
culture like." Include one about the role's success metrics, one about the
team's current challenges, and one about growth.
```

**The strong close (say some version out loud):**
> "This conversation made me even more excited about the role — especially
> [specific thing]. I'm confident I could make a real impact on [their goal]. Is
> there anything about my background I can clarify before you decide?"

**The 24-hour thank-you:**
```
Write a warm, specific thank-you email to [name, title] within 24 hours of my
interview. Reference [one thing we discussed], reaffirm my interest, and add one
sentence of value. Under 120 words.
```

## Part 6 — Salary Negotiation Role-Plays (where this pays for itself)

The 10–15 minutes that can add thousands. Practice them so you're calm and
confident live.

**Deflecting the early salary question:**
> "I'd like to understand the full scope first — I'm confident we can find a
> number that works. What range has the team budgeted for this role?"

**The counter (the core move):**
> "I'm genuinely excited to join. Based on my experience with [achievement] and
> my market research, I was hoping we could get the base closer to [target]. Is
> there flexibility there?" — **then stop talking.**

**The role-play prompt (rehearse against AI):**
```
Role-play as a hiring manager who just offered me [$offer] for [role]. I want to
negotiate to [$target]. Push back realistically — give me the objections a real
manager would ("that's above our band," "the budget is fixed"). Make me practice
holding my number and pivoting to total comp (signing bonus, PTO, title, remote,
early review). After 4–5 exchanges, score how I did and what to improve.
```

**When base won't move — the levers to pull:**
Signing bonus · extra PTO · 6-month review with raise · remote/flex days · title
bump · professional-development budget · earlier equity vesting · start-date
flexibility.

**Get it in writing** before resigning anything. Always.

# 06 — Salary Negotiation Scripts

The 10 minutes that pay the most per minute of your entire career. Most offers
have room, and most people never ask. These are word-for-word scripts plus the
AI prompts to tailor them to your exact number.

---

## Rule zero: never say a number first if you can avoid it

When asked "What are your salary expectations?" early on:

> *"I'd love to learn more about the full scope of the role first. I'm confident
> we can find a number that works for both of us — what range has the team
> budgeted for this position?"*

If pushed for a number, give a **researched range** with your target near the
bottom:

> *"Based on my research for this role and market, I'm targeting somewhere in
> the [$X to $Y] range, depending on the full package."*

---

## Prompt — find your number

```
Help me set a salary target. For a [job title] with [X] years of experience in
[city/remote] and these skills [list], give me:
- A realistic market range (low / mid / high).
- A confident target number to anchor on.
- The 3 strongest justification points I can cite (skills, results, market).
Note: verify against a live salary site before I negotiate.
```

*(Cross-check on a free salary source — Levels.fyi, Glassdoor, LinkedIn Salary,
or Bureau of Labor Statistics — before you commit to a number.)*

---

## The script: when you get the offer

**Step 1 — Always express enthusiasm first.**
> *"Thank you so much — I'm genuinely excited about this role and the team."*

**Step 2 — Ask for time. Never accept on the spot.**
> *"This is a big decision and I want to give it the consideration it deserves.
> Could I have until [day] to review the full offer?"*

**Step 3 — The counter (the core move).**
> *"I'm very excited to join. Based on my experience with [your top relevant
> achievement] and my research on the market for this role, I was hoping we
> could get the base closer to [$your target]. Is there flexibility there?"*

Then **stop talking.** Silence is your tool. Let them respond.

---

## When they say no to more base

Pivot to total compensation — there's almost always another lever:

> *"I understand the base may be fixed. Could we look at [signing bonus / extra
> PTO / earlier review at 6 months / remote flexibility / professional
> development budget / a higher title] instead?"*

---

## Prompt — tailor the counter to your situation

```
Write me a polite, confident salary counter-offer email. I received an offer of
[$offer] for [role] at [company]. I want to counter at [$target]. My strongest
leverage points are [list]. Keep it warm, appreciative, and firm — under 150
words. End by reaffirming I want to join.
```

---

## Prompt — the competing-offer version (if you have one)

```
Rewrite the counter to mention I have another offer at [$amount] but [company]
is my first choice. Make it gracious, not threatening — I want them to match,
not feel cornered.
```

---

## Three truths to hold onto

1. **They expect you to negotiate.** Asking professionally never rescinds an
   offer. It signals confidence.
2. **A $5,000 bump compounds.** It raises every future raise and your next job's
   offer. This conversation is worth tens of thousands over a career.
3. **Get the final offer in writing** before you resign anything.

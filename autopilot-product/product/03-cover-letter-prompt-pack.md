# 03 — The Cover Letter Prompt Pack

A tailored, non-robotic cover letter in under two minutes. The secret is to feed
the AI three things: the job posting, your resume, and a real reason you want
*this* job. The third one is what makes it not sound like every other letter.

---

## The main prompt — your go-to for every application

```
You are a professional cover letter writer. Write a tailored cover letter for
the job posting below. Use my resume for facts — never invent experience.

Structure:
- Opening (2–3 sentences): a specific hook about why THIS company/role, not a
  generic "I'm excited to apply." Reference something real from the posting.
- Body (1–2 short paragraphs): connect my 2 most relevant achievements directly
  to their top requirements, using their language. Show outcomes, not duties.
- Close (2 sentences): confident call to action.

Tone: warm, confident, human. No clichés ("I am writing to express my
interest", "team player", "fast-paced environment"). Under 250 words. Plain
text, ready to paste.

WHY I WANT THIS ROLE (real reason): [one honest sentence]
JOB POSTING: [paste]
MY RESUME: [paste]
```

---

## Variations for specific situations

**Shorter (for an application box or email body):**
```
Rewrite the cover letter above as a 120-word email. Keep the strongest hook and
one achievement. End with a clear ask for a conversation.
```

**Cold outreach (no posting — emailing a hiring manager directly):**
```
Write a 90-word LinkedIn/email message to [name], the [title] at [company]. I
want to be considered for [type of role]. Open with something specific I admire
or noticed about their work/company, connect ONE of my achievements to a problem
they likely have, and ask for a brief chat. Confident, not desperate. No
attachments mentioned.
```

**Referral / warm intro version:**
```
Rewrite the cover letter to mention that [name] suggested I apply / I was
referred by [name]. Put it naturally in the first two sentences.
```

---

## The "make it sound like me" pass

AI letters can sound polished but generic. Fix it in one step:

```
Here's a cover letter you wrote and 2–3 sentences I actually wrote (below).
Rewrite the letter to match MY voice — my rhythm, my word choices, my level of
formality — while keeping it professional and tight.

MY WRITING SAMPLE: [paste a few sentences you wrote naturally — a text, an old
email, anything]
THE LETTER: [paste]
```

---

## Three rules for cover letters that get read

1. **Name the company in the first sentence and say something true about it.**
   Recruiters can smell a find-and-replace letter instantly.
2. **Don't restate your resume — connect it.** The job of the letter is to draw
   the line between *what you did* and *what they need*.
3. **One page, four short paragraphs, always.** Nobody reads more.
